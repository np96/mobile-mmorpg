# Original fortress environment source art

These FBX files are deterministic source inputs for the Session RPG Unity
authoring pipeline. They are generated by
`Tools/Art/Blender/build_fortress_kit.py`; do not repair or rescale an individual
FBX manually.

Asset contract:

| FBX | Blender dimensions (X width, Y depth, Z height) | Triangle cap |
|---|---:|---:|
| `FortressWallStraight.fbx` | 7.72 × 1.50 × 3.20 m | 1,400 |
| `FortressWallBroken.fbx` | 7.72 × 1.50 × 3.20 m | 1,400 |
| `FortressTower.fbx` | 7.72 × 4.20 × 5.20 m | 3,200 |
| `FortressBrazier.fbx` | 1.40 × 1.40 × 2.55 m | 650 |
| `Wardstone.fbx` | visual radius 0.98-1.03 m (authoritative radius 1.05 m), height 2.28-2.35 m | 1,500 |
| `FortressFoundationRing.fbx` | radius 21.30-25.45 m, height -1.80--0.45 m | 8,000 |

All roots and mesh objects use stable ASCII names. Each FBX contains one visual
mesh with UV0. Straight and broken walls use one stone slot. Towers, braziers, and
wardstones use stone at index 0 and emissive ember at index 1. This keeps the
intended twelve-wall/six-tower ring at twenty-four submesh draws. Unity owns shared
materials and all collision/gameplay proxies.

The foundation ring also uses only the stone slot, adding one renderer and one draw
to the complete perimeter.

`FortressKit.manifest.json` is generated evidence containing the actual bounds and
counts from the same Blender run that emitted the FBXs, including the actual
maximum planar vertex radius used to protect the wardstone gameplay footprint.
The wardstone is a broad stacked octagonal masonry cover: three chunky body
courses between a wide base and cap, with 8-12 cm structural bevels and one small
ember inset. It deliberately avoids the narrow slit-pylon silhouette that was
unreadable in the strategy camera.

The foundation ring is a single joined, closed, visual-only stone mesh. Its
continuous submerged shoulder and broad bevel profile connect the eighteen sunk
wall/tower roots; eighteen inset inner bays and eighteen radial feet preserve the
module rhythm. Every vertex remains outside radius 21.25 m and inside 25.50 m. Its
highest point is approximately -0.45 m and its base is approximately -1.80 m, so it
cannot read as playable ground and it leaves continuous visible lava between the
15.0 m arena edge and its 21.30 m inner edge. The ring has no collider, rig, light,
camera, or gameplay authority. Exact target-camera evidence and round-trip metrics
are generated under `../outputs/art/blender/fortress-kit/`.

## Arena floor visual

`ArenaFloorVisual.fbx` is a separate first-party visual mesh generated by
`Tools/Art/Blender/build_arena_floor.py`. It has an exact 15 m radius and visual top
at 0 m, uses no more than 12 cm of recessed relief, and contains no collider,
armature, camera, light, or gameplay authority. Its carved rings, center medallion,
beveled rim, and meter-scale flagstone sectors remain one renderer with exactly
three material slots. Its five broad masonry bands use shared irregular boundaries
and non-aligned seams; only the medallion, two narrow inlays, and outer pinstripe use
Trim. Exact 30 m planar UV0 maps the disk once to the 1024 px macro BaseMap; there
is no overlay renderer. The original micro normal and packed mask retain their
6.4 m repeat through independent `4.6875` tiling and a phase-preserving offset.
All texture paths, hashes, transforms, and the gameplay-camera proof are documented
in the asset manifest. The authoritative flat arena surface remains Unity-owned.

After generating or updating the FBX, run
`Tools/Agent/unity.sh configure-arena-floor` once before rebuilding the playable
foundation. Unity validates the exact one-mesh, three-slot Stone/Trim/Grout order
and assigns project-owned materials; it never imports embedded Blender materials.
